MEMORY & GLOBAL CHIPS

Memory was the worst group on the board again, and the efficiency question is the thread to watch

Memory and storage fell 3.57%, the weakest group on either sheet, with SanDisk down 5.40% and Western Digital down 5.36% while Micron finished essentially flat at +0.06%. SanDisk remains the most technically damaged name on the board by a wide margin: eleven distribution days, 20.66% below its 50-day average, and at just 37% of its 30-day range. It reported fourth-quarter results after today's close, so that reaction is not yet in these prices. The fundamental case has not changed and remains genuinely strong: DRAM contract prices are set to rise 50% to 55% this quarter against the fourth quarter of 2025, HBM is effectively sold out for 2026 with Micron's capacity committed through 2027, and Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron control over 95% of global DRAM while having reallocated capacity toward high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators. The counter-argument that deserves attention is efficiency. Compression research aimed at shrinking the memory footprint of large AI models, most prominently Google's TurboQuant work published in the spring, has already prompted analysts to revisit DRAM demand forecasts more than once. The considered view from the industry press is that it is a meaningful optimisation rather than a solution, since it does not reduce model size, training cost, networking or storage, and one analysis argued it could raise memory demand overall by making AI inference cheaper to deploy widely.

Why it matters This is the most uncomfortable and most common situation a long-term investor faces: the fundamentals say earnings are coming, the chart says institutions have been selling for weeks, and both are true at once. The reconciliation is usually time rather than a clean bottom. What is worth genuinely watching, as opposed to worrying about, is the efficiency thread, because it is the one development that could break the memory thesis rather than merely delay it. If models get cheaper to run faster than AI demand grows, the shortage ends early. That is an argument for owning memory inside a broad chip basket such as SOXX or SMH, which hold Micron, SanDisk and Western Digital alongside Nvidia and Broadcom, rather than concentrating in the memory makers themselves. SK Hynix, the HBM leader, trades only in Seoul, so a basket is the only route most US investors have to it anyway.

PRIME BROKERAGE

Leverage is back near records, and the cushion that made the last bounce easy has largely been spent

Reported Goldman Sachs prime-brokerage data describes a full round trip completed over about eight weeks. Through mid-July, hedge funds sold US technology at a record pace, acting as net sellers in six of eight weeks, with mega-cap tech exposure falling to its lowest point of the year and semiconductors leading the outflows. Since then the money has come back: net long exposure to global information technology posted its biggest increase in more than five years, and positioning is back near the highest levels since the bank began tracking these trades in 2016. The leverage picture is the part worth dwelling on. Gross leverage across the full prime book rose for a third consecutive year to a record by the end of 2025, and net leverage now sits near three-year highs, with US long-short net leverage reported at 55.3%, in the top 11% of readings over the past year. Magnificent 7 names account for roughly 19% of total US net exposure, up from about 11% in early April though still below the 21% peak of mid-2024. Alongside the re-leveraging, higher inflation readings and climbing bond yields have pushed managers to add downside protection, so hedging activity has risen even as the long positions were rebuilt.

Why it matters Positioning explains a great deal of what this board has done and what it is likely to do next. An emptied book is why a chip rally could run on half the usual volume a week ago; a rebuilt book is why today's decline needed so little selling pressure to happen. When leverage is near records and the crowd is concentrated in the same mega-cap names, the market becomes more sensitive to any shock, because leveraged holders are forced to sell rather than choosing to. That is not a forecast of a decline, and crowded positioning can stay crowded for a long time. Primer: how AI infrastructure makes money, layer by layerexpand

Primer: How AI Infrastructure Makes Money

A plain-English tour of what actually goes into an AI data center and where the dollars land, layer by layer. No jargon required.

A large AI data center is a machine built in physical layers, and money flows into it from the top (the customers who order it) down through every supplier who makes a piece. Here is where the dollars land, and why the least glamorous layers can be the safest way to invest.

Start at the top: the customers

The hyperscalers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle) announce the giant spending budgets, now roughly $725B in 2026, that everyone else competes to capture. Owning them is the most diversified way to own the theme, because their core businesses (ads, cloud, software) fund the AI bet and keep earning even if AI spending pauses. Two cautions: the market now scrutinizes the return on that capex, and the group is increasingly tapping external financing, which makes the whole chain more rate-sensitive. Meta's latest raise was attributed to higher component prices rather than more capacity, which is a cost increase dressed as an investment increase.

Layer 1: Silicon, the brains

Nvidia (NVDA) designs the GPUs that do the AI math; AMD (AMD) is the challenger; Broadcom (AVGO) builds the custom chips hyperscalers increasingly design in-house, a trend now challenging Nvidia's near-monopoly. None own factories: TSMC (TSM) prints the chips using machines only ASML (ASML) makes, and every GPU needs memory from Micron (MU). Highest growth, highest risk, and the layer that falls first on any overcapacity or competition scare. It is also now the layer priced for perfection: AMD grew data-center revenue 107% and the stock still fell 7%.

Layer 1b: Memory, the new bottleneck

Memory deserves its own line. Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron control over 95% of global DRAM and have shifted a large majority of combined output to high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators. That has sent AI memory pricing sharply higher and left consumer-grade memory scarce, which is a margin tailwind for MU, SNDK and WDC and a cost shock for every device maker. It makes this the most cyclical layer of the buildout. The live question is efficiency: compression techniques that shrink the memory footprint of AI models could either soften the shortage or, by making inference cheaper, spread AI workloads widely enough to increase memory demand overall. SK Hynix is Seoul-listed, so US investors reach it mainly through the baskets.

Layer 2: The box and the room

The chips get assembled into servers by Super Micro (SMCI) and Dell (DELL). A rack of AI chips runs blazing hot, so it needs advanced cooling and power distribution from Vertiv (VRT), and fast switches from Arista (ANET) to connect racks together. This is the guts of the room, and these are project businesses whose revenue lands unevenly quarter to quarter, which is why single-day moves of 10% or more are routine here in both directions.

Layer 3: Moving the data

AI splits one job across thousands of chips, so they must talk at the speed of light over fiber. Transceivers and lasers come from Coherent (COHR) and Lumentum (LITE), long-haul interconnect from Ciena (CIEN), and the actual glass fiber from Corning (GLW). Optics content rises with every hardware generation as 800G gives way to 1.6T. This layer is now also a policy battleground, with a draft US rule targeting Chinese-made optical modules, which cuts both ways: it hands domestic suppliers pricing power while exposing how much capacity sits offshore.

Layer 4: The electrical bones

The least glamorous and often most attractive layer: switchgear from Eaton (ETN) and Hubbell (HUBB), the steel and PVC conduit that carries every wire from Atkore (ATKR), jobsite supply from WESCO (WCC), transmission and substation build from Quanta (PWR), and the raw steel from Nucor (NUE). Physical, hard-to-substitute, multi-year backlogs. This was the only Sheet 1 group to finish higher today, and five of its six names closed at or near the top of their 30-day ranges.

Layer 5: The power

A data center is useless without electricity, and AI has made power the true bottleneck. Existing-reactor operators signing long deals are Constellation (CEG), Vistra (VST) and Talen (TLN); the regulated fleet owners are Dominion (D), Southern (SO), PSEG (PEG) and Entergy (ETR); fuel comes from Cameco (CCJ) and Centrus (LEU); reactor and SMR technology from BWX (BWXT), Oklo (OKLO) and NuScale (SMR); and near-term gas-turbine and grid power from GE Vernova (GEV). Because the regulated names are effectively bond proxies, this layer trades against the 10-year yield as much as against the AI story, which is why it has been the weakest part of Sheet 1 while yields climbed.

Chips versus picks-and-shovels

Follow a dollar of capex: it lands most heavily on the chip layer, which captures the most dollars but carries the most risk (a rival can win a socket, custom silicon can erode the moat, and an overcapacity or return-on-investment scare hits it first). The power, conduit, cooling and fiber layers are lower-binary: whatever chip and model win, the building still needs steel, copper, transformers and megawatts. That is the trade-off, highest upside in silicon, more durable margins of safety in the infrastructure beneath it. Today was a clean illustration: chips, memory and photonics all fell while electrical and steel rose.

Field Guide: The Numbers That Decide a Long-Term Buy

Every metric on the board, what high and low normally mean, and the threshold each Bull Score check uses. These are also the hover tooltips on every column.

P/E & Fwd P/E The price of a dollar of profit

P/E is the share price divided by yearly profit per share: literally the dollars you pay for each dollar the company earns. A LOW P/E (under ~15) normally means the stock is cheap OR the market doubts the profits will last (value trap risk). A HIGH P/E (over ~30) normally means the market expects strong growth and you are pre-paying for it; if the growth disappoints, the multiple deflates hard (this month's AI selloff was exactly that). Forward P/E uses next year's expected profit: when it is far below the trailing P/E, analysts expect earnings to grow into the price. Always compare within an industry, and pair with PEG before judging.

check: Fwd P/E below trailing P/E = earnings expected to grow

PEG The growth-adjusted price tag

P/E divided by the expected profit growth rate. It answers the question P/E alone cannot: is the growth worth the price? Around 1.0 is fair; under 1.0 you are getting growth at a discount; over 2.0 you are paying up on faith.

check: PEG ≤ 1.5 · green ≤ 1.0 · amber > 2.0

ROE The quality engine

Profit per dollar of shareholder capital. This is the compounding engine of a long-term holding: 10-20% is strong, sustained above 20% is elite, negative means the company burns money. High ROE with low debt is the quality signature.

check: ROE ≥ 10% · red if negative

D/E The survival test

Debt versus the shareholders' own money. Low debt means the company controls its fate through downturns and high-rate periods; high debt means refinancing risk decides for it. Matters double while the Fed is hawkish.

check: D/E ≤ 1.5 · green ≤ 0.5

vs 200D The long-term trend

Distance from the 200-day moving average, the classic dividing line between long-term uptrend and downtrend. Above it, time is on your side; below it, you are fighting the tide (fine for slow accumulation of quality, bad for lump sums). More than 10% below is serious trend damage.

check: price above the 200-day · red < -10%

D-Days Is big money leaving?

Distribution days: sessions in the last 25 that fell at least 0.2% on above-average volume, the footprint of institutions selling into the market. A cluster warns you even when price looks fine. ⚠ flags a heavy dump (down ≥1.5% on ≥1.5x volume) within the last 5 sessions.

check: fewer than 5 and no ⚠ · amber at 3-4

How many of the six checks above pass right now. 5-6: the data supports a bull case (valuation, quality, safety, trend and flows all agree). 3-4: mixed, the thesis needs a specific reason. 0-2: the data argues against it; if you still want it, you are making a contrarian bet and should size it that way.
